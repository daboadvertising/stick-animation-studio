# Open Loops: retention-first scriptwriting

An **open loop** is a specific question the script plants in the viewer's mind and deliberately leaves unanswered for a while, then pays off. A loop has three parts: **OPEN** (a precise question or promise), **DELAY** (the story continues while it hangs) and **CLOSE** (a satisfying, true answer).

## Why it works (what the evidence actually supports)
- **Information-gap theory (Loewenstein, 1994):** curiosity is a felt gap between what you know and what you want to know.
  - People need *some* prior knowledge to feel curious: you can't be curious about something you know nothing about. So give context first, then open the gap.
  - Curiosity peaks when the answer feels *close but not yet known* (an inverted U). Gaps that are too vague or too big don't pull.
- **Interrupted tasks:** a 2025 meta-analysis (Ghibellini & Meier) found:
  - The famous **Zeigarnik effect** (better *memory* for unfinished tasks) is **not reliable**.
  - The **Ovsiankina effect** (a strong urge to *resume* an interrupted task) **is** robust.
  - So the honest claim is: "people want to go back and finish what was interrupted", not "people remember unfinished things better".
- **Creator practice** (practitioner advice, not research): open loops paired with a change of pace roughly every 60–90 s; every loop must be closed; overuse reads as spam or clickbait. Treat timing rules as starting points and check them against your YouTube retention graphs.

## Loop types (use several, layered)
| Type | Span | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Promise loop** (master loop) | Opened ≤30 s, closed in the last 10–15% | Holds the whole video | "Two of those Spartans walked away alive. By the end you'll know what happened to them." |
| **Cold-open loop** | Start at the climax, then rewind | Instant stakes | Ugarit's king dictating while his city burns → "Rewind 150 years." |
| **Mystery loop** | 2–6 min | A story-level unknown | "A discovery in 2020 changed the question entirely." |
| **Countdown loop** | Whole video (lists) | Viewers stay for #1 | "Number five… number one" + "one question that breaks them all" |
| **Mini-loop** | 5–90 s | Gets past dips in the middle | "And then a man walked into the Persian camp." (answered next line) |
| **Bridge loop** | Around subscribe asks and section changes | Stops exits at hand-offs | "Subscribe, then stay, because Egypt is about to fight for its life." |
| **Visual loop** | 5–30 s | Something on screen is unexplained until the narration catches up | A blurred, locked card; a red dotted path drawn before it's explained |
| **Twist loop** | Short | Flips what was just resolved | "But here's the twist. Winning didn't save Egypt." |

## Placement map: long-form (≈1,750 words, 10–12 min)
- **0:00–0:30:** cold open plus the **promise loop** (specific and concrete), plus one quick mini-loop.
- **~20–25%:** first subscribe ask, wrapped in a **bridge loop** ("…because the next part is where this turns").
- **25–60%:** layer **mystery loops** and **mini-loops**. Rule: **always open a new loop before closing the current one** (overlapping, like roof tiles), so there's never a moment with nothing pending.
- **~30–40% (the usual mid-video dip):** a **twist** or a new mystery loop at the exact spot attention sags.
- **~60–65%:** second subscribe ask plus a bridge loop into the climax.
- **Last 15%:** close the mystery loops; close the **promise loop last** (it's the payoff people waited for).
- **Final 10 s:** a question for the comments (a soft open loop for discussion) plus subscribe. Never introduce an unanswered *story* loop at the end of a long video.

## Placement map: Shorts (45–60 s, hook-for-the-main-video)
- **0–3 s:** a pattern-break loop ("Everything you know about the 300 is wrong.").
- **3–40 s:** setup plus 2–3 mini-loops that open and close quickly (they give momentum and small payoffs).
- **40–55 s:** **open the main video's biggest loop and do NOT close it** ("What did he choose? …it's on the channel now"). The Short is the OPEN; the long video is the CLOSE.
- The last line should loop smoothly back into the first line.

## Writing rules
1. **Be specific.** Specific loops pull; vague teases are ignored. Use names, numbers, places and stakes. Weak: "something shocking happens". Strong: "two of the 300 walked away alive".
2. **Give context before the gap.** A loop needs enough knowledge to care (information-gap theory).
3. **Every loop closes.** Keep a ledger. An unclosed story loop breaks trust and hurts comments.
4. **The payoff must be worth the wait, and true.** StickSense accuracy rule: never tease something the sources don't support; never exaggerate the answer.
5. **Layer, don't stack.** Have one or two big loops open at a time, plus quick mini-loops. More than three "stay to the end" promises feels spammy.
6. **Close in the same terms you opened.** If you promised "what happened to the two survivors", answer exactly that.
7. **Vary the wording.** Don't use "stay to the end" more than once. Use "by the end you'll know…", "remember that letter…", "that's where this turns".
8. **Callbacks close loops beautifully.** Return to the opening image ("Now go back to that king by the sea.").
9. **Visual support (StickSense animation):** show a locked or blurred card or a "?" when a loop opens, and reveal it when it closes. Use a recurring on-screen reminder for the promise loop only if it isn't distracting.

Times: estimate at the user's pace (≈2.8 spoken words per second → 1,750 words ≈ 10.5 min).

## Final checklist
- [ ] A promise loop in the first 30 s, specific and true
- [ ] A cold open or a strong hook in the first 5 s
- [ ] A new loop opens before the previous one closes (no "dead air" over 60–90 s)
- [ ] A twist or mystery placed at the ~30–40% dip
- [ ] Bridge loops around both subscribe asks
- [ ] Every loop closed; the promise loop closed last; the payoff matches the promise
- [ ] No fake teases; facts verified (StickSense accuracy rule)
- [ ] Shorts: opens the main video's biggest loop and leaves it open
