# Prompt 2 — Extended voiceover script (~1,750 words, paragraphs)

---
Expand the chosen script into an extended script of **1,750 words in plain paragraph format**: no timestamps,
no headings and no stage directions, because I will read it aloud as the voiceover.

- Keep the hook in the first two sentences, and tease the best part early ("…and #3 is the one most people miss").
- If it's a countdown, start each item with "Sign number five: …", "Step number four: …" and so on.
  The animation tool uses those exact words to cut to a title card.
- Weave in **three natural subscribe call-outs**: one early (who the channel is for), one in the middle
  ("if you're enjoying this…"), and one in the outro ("subscribe to StickSense"). Tie each one to the topic.
- End with a comment question ("Which one was hardest for you? Tell me in the comments.").
- Tone: {calm mentor | intense documentary | curious storyteller}, and **very accurate**.
- After the script, list fact notes: the sources for every study, number or quote you used.
---
Tip: record the voiceover reading the script **exactly** as written. Any wording you change
lowers the alignment score (it still works above about 85%).

## Accuracy rule (always)
Every story must be the real, documented account of events. Real people, dates, places and order of events. Every other script must be accurate to the letter. No invented quotes, stats, studies, names or scenes. Mark approximate dates and debated points as such. Never present a theory as fact. End with a source list. If a claim cannot be sourced, cut it.

## Open loops (always)
Apply the `open-loops` skill (skills/open-loops/SKILL.md). Plan the loops before writing, follow its placement map, and attach a LOOP MAP plus checklist after the script.

## Voice (always)
- **Natural storyteller voice (user feedback: avoid 'robotic' scripts):** write like a person talking to one viewer. Use contractions, 'you' and 'I', honest reactions ('this line genuinely stopped me'), light humour, varied rhythm (fragments plus flowing sentences), and moments rather than lists. Never use stiff transitions such as 'Think about that' or 'Here's something to hold on to'. Facts stay exact.
